\section{Introduction}

The Standard Model (SM) of the elementary particles \cite{Grossman_StandardModel_2023} is known for describing the fundamental particles and their interactions, yet it does not provide a complete description of nature. e.g., it doesn't explain the nature of dark matter, the origin of neutrino masses, and the matter-antimatter asymmetry of the universe. Many Beyond the SM (BSM) theories and models have been developed in an attempt to account for these phenomena as well. Some prominent candidates include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Supersymmetry model, that adds a superpartner for every SM state, as a possible explanation of dark matter.
    \item The seesaw model, adding right handed neutrino fields to have a mass term emerge for them.
    \item Leptogenesis models that, additional to neutrino masses, suggest CP violation to explain matter-antimatter asymmetry.
\end{itemize}

Particle accelerator are the most common tool used in search for BSM physics, due to their ability not only to reach high energies but also huge luminosities. Example are the Large hadron Collider (LHC) \cite{CERN_Courier_LHC_Run3} with its state of the art collision energy of 13.6 TeV and expected total integrated luminosity of $550fb^{-1}$ since it's first collisions, and the foreseen future FCC-ee \cite{Benedikt_2026_FCC} with energy of 90 to 365 GeV and luminosity of $0.67ab^{-1}$ to $68ab^{-1}$ (depending on the interaction point) per year of operation. The hundreds of searches for BSM physics conducted so far at the LHC data have not found any profound evidence for new processes. See, for example \cite{PDG_2024} and references therein. Yet, only a small fraction of possible scenarios have been probed. For example, in ATLAS pMSSM searches, about 40\% points excluded by ATLAS escape the currently available simplified model constraints \cite{Ambrogi_2018_pMSSM_misses}. In the search for BSM physics, powerful particle colliders such as the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) at CERN have been in use, due to their ability to poke into the high energy regime where new physics may manifest itself. Although current searches based upon the LHC data have failed to show any evidence for BSM physics (but have been used to rule out most theoretically motivated BSM models), much rich and complex data has been generated that can be used for future analysis. 

A classic search for a BSM physics model \cite{ATLAS_Higgs_2012} consists of several time-consuming and human-intensive steps, such as formulating selection criteria to enhance the signal of the SM background, modeling the expected signal and background shapes, quantifying potential systematic uncertainties and constructing a solid statistical model. Yet, such steps take an extensive amount of time and labor to execute. 

In our previous works \cite{Volkovich_2022_DDP, Birman_2022_Symmetries, LastDDPPaper} we suggested addressing these limitations with our Data Directed Paradigm for model agnostic searches. By efficiently scanning the detector generated data for deviations from SM-predicted properties, our search could identify possible starting points for future studies. In particular, symmetries respected by the SM set selection rules for, and relations between, different observable processes, and grant us an anchor from which to search for deviations. 

In this paper, we generalize the symmetry method first proposed in \cite{LastDDPPaper}. There, we presented the symmetric formalism, which is a statistical method to test differences between pairs of data samples.

Here, we add mechanisms to cope with simulated data complexities, and use it to test data sample pairs which are predicted to by symmetric by the SM. We added the ability to examine multiple observable quantities and to model nuisance parameters in our system and cancel out their effect on the samples.

We continue our work to look into the lepton sector in search of lepton flavor violations, as it is a well motivated area for BSM searches that fit's our model's abilities. This is done with by using CMS Open Data sample pairs which are symmetric by the lepton flavor of the cuts made to create them.
