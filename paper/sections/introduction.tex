\subsection{Beyond the Standard Model Searches}


The \textbf{Standard Model} (SM) of the elementary particles describes the behavior of high energy physics, yet it does not provide a complete description and does not take into account things like the nature of dark matter, the origin of neutrino masses, and the matter-antimatter asymmetry of the universe. In order to resolve these issues one needs the formulation of \textbf{Beyond Standard Model} (BSM) physics.

In the search for BSM physics powerful particle colliders, such as the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) at CERN, have been in use, due to their ability to poke into the high energy regime where new physics may manifest itself. While current searches based upon the LHC data have failed to show any evidence for BSM physics, the generated data has been accumulated for years and can be used for future analysis of such models. Yet, while the data generated by the LHC is rich and complex, most theoretically motivated BSM models have been ruled out in recent years' research. 

A classic analysis of a model that extends the SM consists of several human-intensive steps, such as speculating a model, formulating predictions to where in the observable parameter space should this model differ from the SM, defining a distinctive experiment, refining cuts, simulating and more. Yet such step may take an extensive amount of time to formulate completely and to test. Nonetheless, our goal is to  suggest a model-agnostic method to search for deviations from the SM predictions, in order to direct future research efforts.

\subsubsection{Search for Lepton Flavor Violating Processes}

One particular section of the SM particles that draws attention to specific searches for new physics is the lepton sector. The SM predicts that leptons of three generations - the electron, the muon and the tau -  each contains a lepton and a neutrino. The foundations of the SM predict three identical generations that are broken only by Yukawa interactions, and in this case we say that there is a \textbf{Lepton Flavor Universality} (LFU) in the SM. However, behaviors such as neutrino oscillations suggest that there is physics we don't yet understand in that sector.

\subsection{In this Paper}

Our goal is to use previously suggested method, the \textbf{Data-Directed Paradigm} (DDP), to look for hints of lepton flavor violating (LFV) evidence in simulated LHC data. To this end, we extend the existing method in several ways:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We look at multi-dimensional data, in terms of physical observables of the events.
    \item We model our knowledge of the effects of a detector (and the potential error term created) using nuisance parameters, which we use to measure its effect on the discovery probability and significance estimation.
    \item We proceed to run on simulated CMS datasets, rather than purely hypothetical samples
\end{enumerate}
