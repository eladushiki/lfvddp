\subsection{Test Statistics}

Wilks and Wald'd theorem gives us an expected behavior of the test statistic we constructed. According to Wilks~\cite{Wilks_Original} and Wald~\cite{Wald_Original}, we have that the distribution of $t$ itself should be $\chi$ squared with of the number of degrees of freedom on our hypothesis:

\begin{equation}
    t \~ \chi^2_{#\text{dof}}
\end{equation}

and so, given no new signal in SR, we should count the number of free parameters in our signal hypothesis and be able to approximate the behavior of $t$.
