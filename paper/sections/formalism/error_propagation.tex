\subsection{Error Propagation in Detector Efficiency}
In practical scenarios, the detector efficiency \( \eta(x) \) is not known but estimated with some uncertainty \( \delta \eta(x) \). This uncertainty propagates into the weights, reconstructed event counts, the test statistic, and the training loss. In addition, since the weights depend inversely on efficiency, then (up to a sign change) we have that $\delta w_i \approx \frac{\delta \eta(x_i)}{\eta(x_i)^2}$, where we have that \(\partial w / \partial \eta = -1/\eta^2\).

Therefore, if the efficiency uncertainties for different events are uncorrelated, then using the standard error propagation formula for independent variables \(\eta(x_i)\), we have that
\[
(\delta \dot{N}_{\mathbf{A}})^2 = \sum_i \left( \frac{\partial \dot{N}_{\mathbf{A}}}{\partial \eta(x_i)} \delta \eta(x_i) \right)^2 = \sum_i \left( -\frac{1}{\eta(x_i)^2} \delta \eta(x_i) \right)^2.
\]


Yet, the test statistic depends on \( \dot{N}_{\mathbf{A}} \), \( \dot{N}_{\mathbf{B}} \), and functions \( f(x), g(x) \) trained on weighted data, and so in the first order, the propagated uncertainty can be approximated as
\[
(\delta t)^2 \approx \left( \frac{\partial t}{\partial \dot{N}_{\mathbf{A}}} \delta \dot{N}_{\mathbf{A}} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{\partial t}{\partial \dot{N}_{\mathbf{B}}} \delta \dot{N}_{\mathbf{B}} \right)^2 + \cdots,
\]
where higher-order contributions and training uncertainties can be estimated numerically. In addition, uncertainty in weights in fact also propagates into the loss:
\[
\delta L = \sqrt{
\sum_{i} \left( \frac{\partial L}{\partial w_i} \delta w_i \right)^2
} = \sqrt{
\sum_{i} \left( \frac{\partial L}{\partial w_i} \frac{\delta \eta(x_i)}{\eta(x_i)^2} \right)^2
}.
\]
A practical approach is to add a regularization term of the form $L_{\mathrm{total}} = L + \lambda \cdot \delta L$, where \(\lambda\) controls the tradeoff between fitting the data and accounting for efficiency uncertainty.

This approach can in fact be estimated using bootstrap consistency. Specifically, if \(t\) is any statistic computed from the efficiency-weighted data and \(\hat{F}\) is the empirical distribution of \((x_i, w_i)\), then the bootstrap variance estimator
\[
\hat{\sigma}^2_{\mathrm{boot}}(t) = \frac{1}{B-1} \sum_{j=1}^B \left( t^{(j)} - \bar{t} \right)^2
\]
converges in probability to \(\mathrm{Var}(t)\) as \(B \to \infty\), provided \(t\) is a smooth functional of \(F\).
