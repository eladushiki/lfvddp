\subsection{Adding Nuisance Parameters to Account for Imperfect knowledge}

We now explain how we can take into account the nuisance parameters that appear in our model, which we use to address the imperfect knowledge of the exact detector efficiency. Even though there are ways to formulate how our theory depends on many different types of imperfections within our model, our model takes into account detector efficiency imperfections that are easily translatable to any other nuisance parameter in the model.

We use the assumption that the nuisance parameters are "small", which adds an additional layer to the calculation of the likelihood. Using a similar argument to that presented in \cite{Italian_no_2_Learning_New_Physics_from_an_Imperfect_Machine}, we claim that the likelihood (which is dependent on some nuisance parameters $\nu_m^j$) becomes

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}\label{eq:nuisance_likelihood_construction}
    \likelihood(\lambda | \dataset{A}, \nparam) &= \likelihood(\dataset{A}| \lambda(\nparam)) \cdot \likelihood(\nparam) \\
    \llikelihood(\lambda | \dataset{A}, \nparam) &= \llikelihood(\dataset{A}| \lambda(\nparam)) + \llikelihood(\nparam)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\noindent while having multiple nuisances sums all their log likelihoods in the same manner.

To estimate the likelihood of a nuisance parameter, we must assume it follows a certain behavioral pattern. For the sake of simplicity, we assume that it follows a normal distribution with some $\sigma_0$ that is centered around $0$. In this case, the likelihood of a gaussian distributed parameter is well known, and can be expressed by

\begin{equation} \label{eq:gaussian_likelihood}
    \likelihood(\nparam) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2 \pi \sigma_0^2}} e^{-\frac{(\nparam)^2}{2 \sigma_0^2}},
\end{equation}

\noindent and whose logarithm can be expressed as  

\begin{equation} \label{eq:gaussian_log_likelihood}
    \llikelihood(\nparam) = -\frac{1}{2} \log(2 \pi \sigma_0^2) - \frac{(\nparam)^2}{2 \sigma_0^2} = - \frac{1}{2 \sigma_0^2} (\nparam)^2 + C
\end{equation}

Plugging \ref{eq:gaussian_likelihood} and \ref{eq:reconstructed_log_likelihood_of_a_distribution} into \ref{eq:nuisance_likelihood_construction} we get the full expression

\begin{equation}\label{eq:ultimate_log_likelihood_of_a_distribution}
\llikelihood(\lambda | \reco{A}, \nparam) =-N_{\reco{A}} + \sum_{\weightedevent \in \reco{A}} w_i f(\event) + N_\reco{A}\log \frac{N_\reco{A}}{Z_f} + \sum_{\weightedevent \in \reco{A}} w_i\log \refdist[\event] - \sum_{\nparam} \frac{1}{2 \sigma_0^2} (\nu^j_m)^2 + C_\reco{A}
\end{equation}
