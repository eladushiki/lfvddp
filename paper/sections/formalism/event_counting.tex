\subsection{Building an Observed Dataset}
\label{sec:building_an_observed_dataset}

During a HEP experiment, large amounts of collision events occur inside the detector. The original number of such events in a dataset is denoted by $\true{N}$.

When detected, some $k$ physical attributes of each event are measured, e.g., energy, momentum components, and angles. Let each event \( \event \coloneq (\phi_1^i, \dots, \phi_k^i) \in \mathbb{R}^k\) be a point in \( k \)-dimensional feature space and each dataset \(\dataset{A} \subset \mathbb{R}^{\true{N} \times k}\) be a collection of $k$ such events.

Due to limited detector efficiency not all events that occur inside the detector are observed. We denote by $\eta(\event)$ the probability with which an event is detected, and hence appears in the observed dataset. We define the observed (filtered) dataset $\meas{A}$ to be the dataset that that stochastically removes events, i.e. the set of all $\event \in \dataset{A}$ that are retained with probability $\eta(\event)$. We denote the observed number of events by $|\meas{A}| = N_\meas{A}$. Our first goal is to estimate \( \true{N} \) from the observed \( N_\meas{A} \), correcting for detector efficiency effects and propagating uncertainties associated with the efficiency estimation.

\subsection{Modeling our Knowledge of the Detector Efficiency}

In our modeling, the datasets statistics are being binned before they can be analyzed. Therefore, as the detectable range is divided into $m^j$ bins along each dimension, we consider the efficiency of the detector $\eta$ and the uncertainty modified efficiency function $\theta$ to be a multiplicative functions in each of the ranges, and thus they are evaluated at a per-bin and per-dimension basis. In addition, we have $\sum_j m^j$ nuisance unknown nuisance parameters $\nu_m^j$ that are considered small for our formulation, yet needed to fully capture the uncertainty effect.


Therefore, we express the (true) efficiency function as a product $\eta(\event) \coloneq \prod_{j=1}^k \eta^j(\phi_i^j)$ where $\eta^j(\phi_i^j) \colon  \mathbb{R} \to [0,1]$, and we express the uncertainty modified efficiency function as $\theta(\event) \coloneq \prod_{j=1}^{k}\theta^j(\phi_i^j)$ where $\theta^j(\phi_i^j) \coloneq e^{-\nu^j_m}\eta^j(\phi_i^j)$ represents our knowledge of the efficiency of each bin.


\subsection{Efficiency Compensation via Event Weights}

To reconstruct the statistics of the original datasets, we assign weights for each event $\event \in \tilde{\mathbf{A}}$ by setting

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:def_weights}
\quad w_i \coloneq \frac{1}{\theta(\event)} = e^{\nu^j_m}\frac{1}{\eta^j(\event)}.
\end{equation}

This gives rise to the efficiency-compensated reconstructed datasets that is given by $\dot{\mathbf{A}} \coloneq \{ (\event, w_i) \colon \event \in \tilde{\mathbf{A}} \}$, whose estimated total event counts is
\[
N_\reco{A} = \sum_{\event \in \meas{A}} w_i \approx \true{N}.
\]

Note that this expression $N_\reco{A}$ includes our knowledge of the efficiency and our error, through the $\eta$'s in the definition of the weights.
