\subsubsection{Constructing a Test Statistic}

To obtain the test statistic that assesses whether sample $\reco{A}$ differs from the pooled reference in the manner parametrized by \(f\), we set the alternative for $B$ to be the pooled baseline (i.e., take \(g\) to represent the baseline, where in the usual symmetrized choice one may take \(g\equiv0\) and absorb the normalization constant into \(Z_B\)). For the sake of simplicity, we take the parametrization where the $B$-component is the baseline with \(g(x)\equiv 0\) (equivalently, measure \(f\) relative to the $B$ baseline). With this choice, there exists some constant $c$ such that 
\[
\log\mathcal{L}_{\text{pooled}}(f) =c \left( -\dot{N} + \sum_{\event\in\mathcal{P}} w_i \log\Big(\alpha \tfrac{1}{Z_A}e^{f(\event)} + (1-\alpha)\tfrac{1}{Z_B}\Big)\right).
\]
By expanding the logarithm and factoring the constants out of the argument we have that
\[
\log\Big(\alpha \tfrac{1}{Z_A}e^{f} + (1-\alpha)\tfrac{1}{Z_B}\Big) = \log\Big(\tfrac{1}{Z_B}\Big) + \log\Big((1-\alpha) + \alpha\tfrac{Z_B}{Z_A}e^{f}\Big),
\]
\noindent where \(\alpha=\dfrac{N_\reco{A}}{N_\reco{A}+N_\reco{B}}\). Therefore, if we denote the ratio \(\rho:=\tfrac{Z_B}{Z_A}\) we can rewrite the remaining term as
\[
\log\Big((1-\alpha) + \alpha\rho e^{f}\Big).
\]
Now, form the log-likelihood ratio (or equivalently the difference between the pooled log-likelihood and the product of the separate log-likelihoods under the null) and collect terms that depend on \(f\). The algebraic manipulations (expand and regroup the pooled-sum and the individual sums) produce two types of contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A term proportional to \(\int \alpha (\rho e^{f}-1) n_{\mathcal{R}}(x)dx\), which, after replacing integrals with weighted sums, yields \(-\alpha\sum_{\mathcal{P}} w_i (e^{f(\event)}-1)\) up to multiplicative constants and absorbed \(\rho\) factors. The appearance of \(e^{f}-1\) is a consequence of subtracting the baseline intensity.
    \item A sum over events in sample A giving \(\sum_{\event\in\tilde{\mathbf{A}}} w_i f(\event)\) originating from the \(\sum\log e^{f}=\sum f\) contribution of labeled-A events when comparing labeled likelihood terms.
\end{enumerate}
These two pieces are the ones that appear in the convex functional minimized to obtain the profile likelihood.

\subsection{Profiling and Simplification to the Displayed Functional}
Carrying out the algebra carefully (expand the pooled log, subtract the baseline contributions, cancel constants and terms involving \(\log Z_A,\log Z_B\) that do not depend on \(f\) after profiling), and using the unbiased replacement of integrals by weighted sums, one obtains the functional
\[
\mathcal{F}(f) = -\alpha \sum_{\event\in\reco{P}} w_i\big(e^{f(\event)}-1\big) + \sum_{\event\in\reco{A}} w_i f(\event) + \text{(constants independent of $f$)}.
\]
The test statistic is the negative twice the maximized log-likelihood ratio (or, equivalently, \(-2\) times the minimum of \(\mathcal{F}(f)\) over allowable functions \(f\)), which yields the stated expression:
\[
t_{\dataset{A}+\dataset{B}}(\dataset{A}) = -2 \min_f \Big[ -\alpha \sum_{\event\in\reco{P}} w_i (e^{f(\event)}-1) + \sum_{\event\in\reco{A}} w_i f(\event) \Big].
\]

\begin{remark}
    \textup{The steps above are algebraic and exact up to the choice of baseline parametrization (we set \(g\equiv 0\) for the baseline without loss of generality) and the unbiased substitution of integrals by weighted sums (which is exact in expectation and valid as an estimating equation). The resulting functional is convex in the pointwise values \(f(\event)\) because the map \(f\mapsto e^{f}-1\) is convex; this justifies numerical minimization via deterministic optimization or training a neural network with the corresponding loss.}
\end{remark}
