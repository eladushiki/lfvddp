\subsubsection{Remodeling Physical Process Sampling}

Our hypothetical accelerator causes particles to collide, creating the sets $\true{A}$ and $\true{B}$ of events. Each $\event \in \mathbb{R}^k$ is represented as a vector of its measured their physical quantities, and is assumed to be the result of an inhomogeneous Poisson process with intensity of either

\begin{equation}   
\begin{aligned}
    \dist{\true{A}}={N_{\true{A}}} p(\anyevent)\\
    \dist{\true{B}}={N_{\true{B}}} p(\anyevent)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $p(\anyevent)$ is the theoretical probability density of the physical process we measure. Using items similar to the original formulation to express $p$, we restate this as:

\begin{eqnarray}
\label{eq:learnable_distribution}
\begin{aligned}
    \dist{\true{A}}_\param = N_{\true{A}} e^{\paramf(\anyevent)} \refdist \\
    \dist{\true{B}}_\param = N_{\true{B}} e^{\paramg(\anyevent)} \refdist
\end{aligned}
\end{eqnarray}

\noindent For now, $f$ and $g$ are arbitrary and are composed from parameters $\param$ that are of interest for us.

This gives us directly the n-dimensional form of our hypotheses:

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \nullhypothesis &: \quad
    \begin{cases}
        \dist{\true{A}}
        &=
        \frac{N_{\true{A}}}
            {\int \refdist\,d\anyevent}
        e^{h(\anyevent)} \refdist,
        \\
        \dist{\true{B}}
        &=
        \frac{N_{\true{B}}}
            {\int \refdist\,d\anyevent}
        e^{h(\anyevent)+r} \refdist,
    \end{cases} \\
    \hypothesis{1} &: \quad
    \begin{cases}
        \dist{\true{A}}
        &=
        \frac{N_{\true{A}}}
            {\int \refdist\,d\anyevent}
        e^{f(\anyevent)} \refdist,
        \\
        \dist{\true{B}}
        &=
        \frac{N_{\true{B}}}
            {\int \refdist\,d\anyevent}
        e^{g(\anyevent)} \refdist.
    \end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
