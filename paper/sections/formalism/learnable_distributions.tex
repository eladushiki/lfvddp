\subsection{Modeling Physical Process Sampling}
Our hypothetical accelerator causes particles to collide, creating the set $\true{A}$ of events, $\event \in \mathbb{R}^k$, which are represented as a vector of some of their physical quantities. We claim that the resulting events are the result of an inhomogeneous Poisson process with intensity

\begin{equation}   
    \lambda(\anyevent)={N_{\true{A}}} p(\anyevent),
\end{equation}

\noindent where $p(\anyevent)$ is the theoretical probability density of the physical process we measure. In addition, we introduce a common reference number density function (NDF), $\refdist$, and parametrize

\begin{equation}
    p(\anyevent) = e^{f(\anyevent)} \refdist,
\end{equation}

\noindent Drawing from our probability density $p_{\true{A}}$, the intensity becomes

\begin{eqnarray}
\label{eq:learnable_distribution}
    \lambda_\param = N_{\true{A}} e^{f(\anyevent)} \refdist.
\end{eqnarray}

For now, $f$ is arbitrary and is composed from parameters that are of interest for us, $\param$. It gives us the freedom to shape the reference NDF.
