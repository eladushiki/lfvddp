\section{Introduction}

The \gls{sm} of elementary particles~\cite{Grossman_StandardModel_2023} describes fundamental particles and their interactions. However, it leaves several questions unanswered, including the nature of dark matter, the origin of neutrino masses, and the matter--antimatter asymmetry of the universe. Many \gls{bsm} theories and models have been proposed to address these questions. Examples include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Supersymmetric models, which introduce a superpartner for each \gls{sm} state and can provide dark matter candidates.
    \item Seesaw models, which introduce right-handed neutrino fields to generate neutrino masses.
    \item Leptogenesis models, which connect neutrino masses and CP violation to the matter--antimatter asymmetry.
\end{itemize}

Particle accelerators combine high collision energies with high luminosities and are therefore a major tool for \gls{bsm} searches. The Large Hadron Collider (LHC)~\cite{CERN_Courier_LHC_Run3}, for example, operates at a collision energy of $13.6\,\mathrm{TeV}$, with an expected total integrated luminosity of $550\,\mathrm{fb}^{-1}$ since its first collisions. The proposed \gls{fcc-ee}~\cite{Benedikt_2026_FCC} would cover energies from $90$ to $365\,\mathrm{GeV}$, with annual integrated luminosities from $0.67$ to $68\,\mathrm{ab}^{-1}$, depending on the interaction point.

Hundreds of searches using LHC data have found no compelling evidence for \gls{bsm} processes; see, for example, Ref.~\cite{PDG_2024} and the references therein. Yet, only a small fraction of possible scenarios has been probed. About $40\%$ of the \gls{pmssm} parameter points excluded by ATLAS, for example, evade the available simplified-model constraints~\cite{Ambrogi_2018_pMSSM_misses}. The volume and complexity of collider data leave considerable scope for further searches.

A conventional search for a specific \gls{bsm} model~\cite{ATLAS_Higgs_2012} requires identifying production mechanisms and decay modes, selecting events to enhance the signal relative to the \gls{sm} background, modeling the signal and background distributions, quantifying systematic uncertainties, and constructing a statistical model. These steps demand substantial time and effort, while each search probes a limited range of possible scenarios.

To address these limitations, we proposed the \gls{ddp} for model-agnostic searches in previous work~\cite{Volkovich_2022_DDP, Birman_2022_Symmetries, LastDDPPaper}. The approach scans detector data for deviations from \gls{sm} predictions to identify starting points for further investigation. In particular, \gls{sm} symmetries impose selection rules and relations between observable processes. These relations provide a reference for testing deviations. The symmetrized formalism of Ref.~\cite{LastDDPPaper} implements this comparison statistically for pairs of data samples.

Here, we extend the formalism to multiple observables and introduce nuisance parameters to account for systematic effects on the samples.

We demonstrate these extensions using CMS Open Data simulated samples in a search for lepton flavor violation. This is a well-motivated target for \gls{bsm} searches and is suited to the symmetry-based approach: samples selected by lepton flavor are compared in pairs expected to respect the corresponding \gls{sm} symmetry.
