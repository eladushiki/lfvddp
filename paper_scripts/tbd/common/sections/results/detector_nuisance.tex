\subsection{Accounting for Detector Imperfections}

Next, we introduce nuisance parameters that are fit to account for systematic uncertainties, and induced imperfect detector effects.

First, we add the adjustable nuisance terms to the hypotheses expressions (Eqs.~\ref{eq:sr_hypothesis_statement} and~\ref{eq:cr_hypothesis_statement}):

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:detector_effect_explicit}
    \delta(\eventsymbol) = \dfrac{1}{2}tanh(\eventsymbol) + \dfrac{1}{2}
\end{equation}

Adding no degrees of freedom to the difference between the hypothesis spaces, we expect a convergence to a $\chi^2$ of an equivalent degree as in Figure~\ref{fig:t_distribution_plot_reproduction}. Indeed, in Figure~\ref{fig:blank-background-with-nuisance} we can observe such behavior.

\begin{figure}
    \addgraph{common/sections/results/plot03-nuisance-enabled-background-only-t-dist-v7/t_distribution_plot_5965431250503226201.png}
    \caption{The same background only distribution of $t$ values with nuisance parameter fitting enabled (see Figure~\ref{fig:t_distribution_plot_reproduction}.}
    \label{fig:blank-background-with-nuisance}
\end{figure}


We now add a slightly imperfect detector function as it acts on datasets of type $\dataset{A}$. As before, we allow nuisance parameters to be adjusted in the process such that they would be learned and compensate for the differences. In Figure~\ref{fig:detector_effect_with_nuisance_train} we observe an example of a product of a single run of our method. A single fit is made to maximize hypothesis of each space simultaneously, and their estimation of the distribution is illustrated on the partial and reference histograms.

\begin{figure}
    \addgraph{common/sections/results/plot03.1-nuisance-enabled-background-only-single-run-v7/dataset_process_plot_-7909826948462366198.png}
    \caption{An example of a train result with a detector efficiency as in Eq.~\ref{eq:detector_effect_explicit} for dataset $\dataset{A}$ (and perfect efficiency for $\dataset{B}$). The nuisance function is the best fit of 1-4-1 fully connected neural network. Top: normalized distributions of the \gls{sr}, \gls{cr}, together with the maximized hypotheses' predictions. Bottom left: both hypotheses' predictions over the \gls{sr} (as in Eq.~\ref{eq:sr_hypothesis_statement}). Bottom right: comparison of their predictions on the \gls{cr} (see Eq.~\ref{eq:cr_hypothesis_statement}).}
    \label{fig:detector_effect_with_nuisance_train}
\end{figure}


In Figure~\ref{fig:detector_affected_significances} we then see a similar illustration of the estimated signal intensities to Figure~\ref{fig:observed_vs_injected_significances}, with induced detector effect as in Equation~\ref{eq:detector_effect_explicit}, and a fitted nuisance function.
