We next introduce fitted nuisance parameters to account for systematic uncertainties and imposed detector imperfections.

We first include adjustable nuisance terms in the hypotheses (Eqs.~\ref{eq:sr_hypothesis_statement} and~\ref{eq:cr_hypothesis_statement}):

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:detector_effect_explicit}
    \delta(\eventsymbol) = \dfrac{1}{2}tanh(\eventsymbol) + \dfrac{1}{2}
\end{equation}

The nuisance parameters do not change the difference in dimensionality between the hypothesis spaces. We therefore expect convergence to a $\chi^2$ distribution with the same number of degrees of freedom as in Figure~\ref{fig:t_distribution_plot_reproduction}. Figure~\ref{fig:blank-background-with-nuisance} shows this behavior.

\begin{figure}

    \caption{Background-only distribution of $t$ with nuisance parameter fitting enabled, corresponding to Figure~\ref{fig:t_distribution_plot_reproduction}.}
    \label{fig:blank-background-with-nuisance}
\end{figure}

We then apply a slightly imperfect detector response to $\dataset{A}$. The nuisance parameters are fitted to account for the resulting differences between the samples. Figure~\ref{fig:detector_effect_with_nuisance_train} shows a single run, in which the two hypotheses are fitted simultaneously. Their predictions are overlaid on the individual-sample and reference histograms.

\begin{figure}
