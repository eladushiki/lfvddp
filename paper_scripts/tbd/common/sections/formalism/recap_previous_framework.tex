In Ref.~\cite{LastDDPPaper}, the signal and null hypotheses were defined as

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:last_ddp_paper_hypotheses}
\begin{aligned}
    \nullhypothesis &: \quad
    \begin{cases}
        \pdf_{\dataset{A}}(\eventsymbol)
        &=
        \frac{N_{\dataset{A}}}
            {\int \refdist[\eventsymbol]\,d\eventsymbol}
        e^{h(\eventsymbol)} \refdist[\eventsymbol],
        \\
        \pdf_{\dataset{B}}(\eventsymbol)
        &=
        \frac{N_{\dataset{B}}}
            {\int \refdist[\eventsymbol]\,d\eventsymbol}
        e^{h(\eventsymbol)+r} \refdist[\eventsymbol],
    \end{cases} \\
    \hypothesis{1} &: \quad
    \begin{cases}
        \pdf_{\dataset{A}}(\eventsymbol)
        &=
        \frac{N_{\dataset{A}}}
            {\int \refdist[\eventsymbol]\,d\eventsymbol}
        e^{f(\eventsymbol)} \refdist[\eventsymbol],
        \\
        \pdf_{\dataset{B}}(\eventsymbol)
        &=
        \frac{N_{\dataset{B}}}
            {\int \refdist[\eventsymbol]\,d\eventsymbol}
        e^{g(\eventsymbol)} \refdist[\eventsymbol].
    \end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\noindent Here, $\hypothesis{1}$ and $\nullhypothesis$ denote the signal and null hypotheses, respectively. The $\pdf$'s describe the assumed underlying probability distributions of $\dataset{A}$ and $\dataset{B}$. Both are expressed relative to a common distribution, $\refdist[\eventsymbol]$, constructed from the combined sample. This provides a common reference for the comparison, with each event described by a single real number, $\eventsymbol$. The functions $f$ and $g$ describe signal-induced shifts between the distributions; $h$ and $r$ account for a common systematic shift and a relative scale, respectively. The remaining factors set the normalization.

The parameters $f, g, h$ and $r$ define the hypothesis spaces. For a given sample pair, neural networks determine the best fit within each space. As argued in Ref.~\cite{LastDDPPaper}, the optimal values of $h$ and $r$ are analytically zero: the distributions have the same shape ($h$), and the scale factor is absorbed into the normalization ($r$). We therefore omit these terms below.
