Our hypothetical accelerator causes particles to collide, creating the sets $\true{A}$ and $\true{B}$ of events. Each event $\event \in \mathbb{R}^k$ is represented as a vector of its measured physical quantities. Each event set is then assumed to be the result of an inhomogeneous Poisson process with intensity of either

\begin{equation}   
\begin{aligned}
    \dist{\true{A}}={N_{\true{A}}} p(\anyevent)\\
    \dist{\true{B}}={N_{\true{B}}} p(\anyevent)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $p(\anyevent)$ is the theoretical probability density to create an event in the specific point in the parameter space. Using items similar to the original formulation to express $p$, we restate this as:

\begin{eqnarray}
\label{eq:process_probability_distribution}
\begin{aligned}
    \dist{\true{A}}_\param = N_{\true{A}} (1+{\paramf(\anyevent)}) \refdist \\
    \dist{\true{B}}_\param = N_{\true{B}} (1-{\paramf(\anyevent)}) \refdist
\end{aligned}
\end{eqnarray}

\noindent For now, $f$ is arbitrary and is composed of parameters $\param$ that are of interest for us. The former $g$ formulation for the $\dataset{B}$ distribution is replaced with a negative $\paramf$, expressing the symmetric shift from $\refdist$, directly expected from its choice as both the distributions' mean.

\iftrue % MATH NOTE 1  (to hide: change \iftrue to \iffalse)
\par\medskip\noindent{\color{blue}\textbf{[Math note 1 -- Eq.~(3)]}\enspace
The symmetric form $(1\pm f)$ follows from choosing $n_R$ as the mean of
the two distributions only if $n_R$ is the \emph{unweighted} mean
$\frac12(p_A+p_B)$. The pooled sample used in the loss (Eq.~(20),
App.~A) is the weighted mixture $w_Ap_A+w_Bp_B$, with
$w_X=N_X/(N_A+N_B)$. For that reference, normalization requires
$\lambda_B\propto(1-\frac{w_A}{w_B}f)\,n_R$. Normalization also needs
$\int f\,n_R\,d\vec x=0$, which should be stated. Note~3 gives the
consequence.\par}\medskip
\fi % end of MATH NOTE 1

This gives us directly the n-dimensional form of our hypotheses:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:learnable_distribution}
\begin{aligned}
    \nullhypothesis &:\\[-0.5ex]
    &\begin{cases}
        \dist{\true{A}}
        &=
        \frac{N_{\true{A}}}
            {\int \refdist\,d\anyevent}
        \refdist,
        \\
        \dist{\true{B}}
        &=
        \frac{N_{\true{B}}}
            {\int \refdist\,d\anyevent}
        \refdist,
    \end{cases} \\
    \hypothesis{1} &:\\[-0.5ex]
    &\begin{cases}
        \dist{\true{A}}
        &=
        \frac{N_{\true{A}}}
            {\int \refdist\,d\anyevent}
        (1 + f(\anyevent)) \refdist,
        \\
        \dist{\true{B}}
        &=
        \frac{N_{\true{B}}}
            {\int \refdist\,d\anyevent}
        (1 - g(\anyevent)) \refdist.
    \end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
