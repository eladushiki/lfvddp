\subsubsection{Hypotheses}

We now define the full null and alternative hypotheses in Eq~\ref{eq:sr_hypothesis_statement} 

\begin{wideequations}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sr_hypothesis_statement}
\begin{aligned}
    \nullhypothesis |_{SR} &:\\[-0.5ex]
    &\begin{cases}
        {\srdist{\dataset{A}}^0} = N_\srdataset{A} (1 + {\nparam(\anyevent)}) \srrefdist \\
        {\srdist{\dataset{B}}^0} = N_\srdataset{B} (1 - {\nparam(\anyevent)}) \srrefdist
    \end{cases} \\
    \hypothesis{1} |_{SR} &:\\[-0.5ex]
    &\begin{cases}
        {\srdist{\dataset{A}}^1} = N_\srdataset{A} (1 + f(\anyevent)) (1 + {\nparam(\anyevent)}) \srrefdist \\
        {\srdist{\dataset{B}}^1} = N_\srdataset{B} (1 - f(\anyevent)) (1 - {\nparam(\anyevent)}) \srrefdist
    \end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{wideequations}

To which we add, for the control region as in Eq.~\ref{eq:cr_hypothesis_statement}

\begin{wideequations}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cr_hypothesis_statement}
\begin{aligned}
    \nullhypothesis |_{CR} = \hypothesis{1} |_{CR} &:\\[-0.5ex]
    &\begin{cases}
        \crdist{\dataset{A}}^i = N_\crdataset{A} (1 + {\nparam(\anyevent)}) \crrefdist \\
        \crdist{\dataset{B}}^i = N_\crdataset{B} (1 - {\nparam(\anyevent)}) \crrefdist
    \end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{wideequations}

Note that we now have two separate reference distributions, that are built in the same manner - the combined sample in either region.

\iftrue % MATH NOTE 5  (to hide: change \iftrue to \iffalse)
\par\medskip\noindent{\color{blue}\raggedright\textbf{[Math note 5 -- Eq.~(8): the composition $(1\pm f)(1\pm\theta)$ is not closed]}\enspace
Take the balanced reference $\bar n_R=\frac12(p_A+p_B)$. The data then
satisfy $r_A+r_B=2$, with $r_X=p_X/\bar n_R$, while
\[
(1+f)(1+\theta)+(1-f)(1-\theta)=2\,(1+f\theta).
\]
An exact fit therefore needs $f\theta=0$ at every point. Once the CR
fixes $\theta\neq0$, $H_1$ cannot represent a different SR asymmetry.
This costs power and makes $f$ hard to interpret.
 
Minimal fix, by relativistic velocity addition:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_A&=N_A\,\frac{(1+f)(1+\theta)}{1+f\theta}\,\bar n_R,\\
\lambda_B&=N_B\,\frac{(1-f)(1-\theta)}{1+f\theta}\,\bar n_R .
\end{aligned}
\]
This form is closed and reduces to $H_0$ at $f=0$. It reaches any
target asymmetry $a$ with $f=(a-\theta)/(1-a\theta)$, and the combined
asymmetry stays in $(-1,1)$. Note~6 gives an equivalent, simpler form.\par}\medskip
\fi % end of MATH NOTE 5
