Consider two sets of collision events, $\true{A}$ and $\true{B}$, produced by a hypothetical accelerator. Each event $\event \in \mathbb{R}^k$ is represented by a vector of measured physical quantities. We assume that each set follows an inhomogeneous Poisson process, with intensities

\begin{equation}   
\begin{aligned}
    \dist{\true{A}}={N_{\true{A}}} p(\anyevent)\\
    \dist{\true{B}}={N_{\true{B}}} p(\anyevent)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $p(\anyevent)$ is the theoretical probability density for producing an event at a given point in parameter space. Following the original formulation, we express $p$ through

\begin{eqnarray}
\label{eq:process_probability_distribution}
\begin{aligned}
    \dist{\true{A}}_\param = N_{\true{A}} (1+{\paramf(\anyevent)}) \refdist \\
    \dist{\true{B}}_\param = N_{\true{B}} (1-{\paramf(\anyevent)}) \refdist
\end{aligned}
\end{eqnarray}

\noindent The function $f$ is parametrized by the parameters of interest $\param$. The earlier formulation~\cite{LastDDPPaper} used separate deformation functions, $f$ and $g$, for the two samples. Here, the independent deformation of $\dataset{B}$ is replaced by $-\paramf$. The two distributions thus have opposite shifts about their mean, $\refdist$.

The corresponding hypotheses in multiple dimensions are

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:learnable_distribution}
\begin{aligned}
    \nullhypothesis &:\\[-0.5ex]
    &\begin{cases}
        \dist{\true{A}}
        &=
        \frac{N_{\true{A}}}
            {\int \refdist\,d\anyevent}
        \refdist,
        \\
        \dist{\true{B}}
        &=
        \frac{N_{\true{B}}}
            {\int \refdist\,d\anyevent}
        \refdist,
    \end{cases} \\
    \hypothesis{1} &:\\[-0.5ex]
    &\begin{cases}
        \dist{\true{A}}
        &=
        \frac{N_{\true{A}}}
            {\int \refdist\,d\anyevent}
        (1 + f(\anyevent)) \refdist,
        \\
        \dist{\true{B}}
        &=
        \frac{N_{\true{B}}}
            {\int \refdist\,d\anyevent}
        (1 - f(\anyevent)) \refdist.
    \end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
