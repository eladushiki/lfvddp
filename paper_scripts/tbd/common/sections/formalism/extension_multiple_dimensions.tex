Our hypothetical accelerator causes particles to collide, creating the sets $\true{A}$ and $\true{B}$ of events. Each event $\event \in \mathbb{R}^k$ is represented as a vector of its measured physical quantities. Each event set is then assumed to be the result of an inhomogeneous Poisson process with intensity of either

\begin{equation}   
\begin{aligned}
    \dist{\true{A}}={N_{\true{A}}} p(\anyevent)\\
    \dist{\true{B}}={N_{\true{B}}} p(\anyevent)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\noindent where $p(\anyevent)$ is the theoretical probability density to create an event in the specific point in the parameter space. Using items similar to the original formulation to express $p$, we restate this as:

\begin{eqnarray}
\label{eq:process_probability_distribution}
\begin{aligned}
    \dist{\true{A}}_\param = N_{\true{A}} (1+{\paramf(\anyevent)}) \refdist \\
    \dist{\true{B}}_\param = N_{\true{B}} (1-{\paramf(\anyevent)}) \refdist
\end{aligned}
\end{eqnarray}

\noindent The function $f$ is parametrized by the parameters of interest $\param$. In the earlier formulation~\cite{LastDDPPaper}, the two samples had separate deformation functions, $f$ and $g$. Here, we replace the independent deformation of $\dataset{B}$ with the opposite shift, $-\paramf$, expressing symmetric shifts from $\refdist$, the mean of the two distributions.

This gives us directly the n-dimensional form of our hypotheses:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:learnable_distribution}
\begin{aligned}
    \nullhypothesis &:\\[-0.5ex]
    &\begin{cases}
        \dist{\true{A}}
        &=
        \frac{N_{\true{A}}}
            {\int \refdist\,d\anyevent}
        \refdist,
        \\
        \dist{\true{B}}
        &=
        \frac{N_{\true{B}}}
            {\int \refdist\,d\anyevent}
        \refdist,
    \end{cases} \\
    \hypothesis{1} &:\\[-0.5ex]
    &\begin{cases}
        \dist{\true{A}}
        &=
        \frac{N_{\true{A}}}
            {\int \refdist\,d\anyevent}
        (1 + f(\anyevent)) \refdist,
        \\
        \dist{\true{B}}
        &=
        \frac{N_{\true{B}}}
            {\int \refdist\,d\anyevent}
        (1 - f(\anyevent)) \refdist.
    \end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
