\paragraph{Expressing the Hypothesis Spaces}

Previously~\cite{LastDDPPaper}, we used a neural network to express the signal hypothesis space. The arguments for choosing it are straight forward - it is a highly expressive function space, that can fit various signal shapes and strengths, while having a clear and sizable set of parameters, that is its degrees of freedom (which will matter in a bit). The downsize is an overfitting tendency and is only approximately conforming of Wilks and Wald (Section~\ref{sec:Wilks_Wald}), a problem which was briefly discussed in \cite{LastDDPPaper} and is further explained in \iftoggle{ispaper}{Appendix}{Section}~\ref{sec:broken_Wilks_Wald}.

Our 3 layered, fully connected, sigmoid activated 
\gls{nn}'s output is expressed by:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:neural_net_explicit}
    f(\event) = b_{0} + \sum\limits_{\alpha = 1}^{N} \omega_{\alpha} \sigma(\sum\limits_{j = 1}^{k}\omega^j_{\alpha} \event^j + b_\alpha).
\end{equation}

Output is passed through tanh to prevent logarithms in the likelihood expression from diverging.

The \gls{dof} of a hypothesis space spanned by a \gls{nn}, then, goes by

\begin{equation}
    N_\text{DOF} = (k + 2) \times N + 1
\end{equation}

\paragraph{\texorpdfstring{$\chi^2$}{Chi-squared} Degrees of Freedom (DOF)}
When considering the free parameters of either hypothesis space the following calculation follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item In $\hypothesis{1}$, the degrees of freedom are in $f$ and $\nparam$ and $\refdist$.
    \item In $\nullhypothesis$, the degrees of freedom are in $\nparam$ and $\refdist$.
\end{itemize}

When counting the \gls{dof} of a hypothesis, we need to take into consideration those of $f$ and those of $\nparam$. This being said, the behavior of the $t$ distribution depends on the difference between the number of \gls{dof} of the singnal and null spaces. The null hypothesis sapce containing the $\nparam$ \gls{dof} as well, we get that the difference is exactly the \gls{dof} of $f$.
