\paragraph{Building an Observed Dataset}
\label{sec:building_an_observed_dataset}

In a HEP experiment, collisions occur inside a detector. For each detected event, $k$ physical attributes, such as energy, momentum components, and angles, are measured. The detector efficiency is finite, so only a subset of the true events is reconstructed and observed.

Let $\detector(\event)$ denote the probability that an event $\event$ is detected and included in the observed dataset. The observed (filtered) dataset $\dataset{A}$ is obtained by stochastic removal of events: each event $\event \in \dataset{A}$ is retained with probability $\detector(\event)$. The observed event count is denoted by $|\dataset{A}| = N_\dataset{A}$.

\paragraph{Modeling Detector Efficiency as Nuisance Parameters}

The detector response is not known perfectly and can differ between $\dataset{A}$ and $\dataset{B}$. Such differences can break a symmetry of the underlying samples. We account for these effects and their uncertainties as follows.

We define $\nparam(\anyevent)$ as half the difference between the detection probabilities for the two samples at a given point in observable space:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nuisance_from_detector_efficiencies}
    \nparam(\anyevent) \coloneqq \frac{\detector_{A}(\anyevent) - \detector_{B}(\anyevent)}{2}   
\end{equation}

Relative to the reference distribution, the detected samples are then expected to take the form

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \dist{\dataset{A}} &= N_{\dataset{A}}(1 + \nparam(\anyevent))\refdist \\
    \dist{\dataset{B}} &= N_{\dataset{B}}(1 - \nparam(\anyevent))\refdist
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The hypotheses including detector efficiency are therefore

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \nullhypothesis &:\\[-0.5ex]
    &\begin{cases}
        \dist{\dataset{A}}
        &=
        N_{\dataset{A}}
        (1 + \nparam(\anyevent)) \refdist,
        \\
        \dist{\dataset{B}}
        &=
        N_{\dataset{B}}
        (1 - \nparam(\anyevent))\refdist,
    \end{cases} \\
    \hypothesis{1} &:\\[-0.5ex]
    &\begin{cases}
        \dist{\dataset{A}}
        &=
        N_{\dataset{A}}
        (1 + f(\anyevent)) (1 + \nparam(\anyevent))\refdist,
        \\
        \dist{\dataset{B}}
        &=
        N_{\dataset{B}}
        (1 - f(\anyevent)) (1 - \nparam(\anyevent))\refdist.
    \end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Under the null hypothesis, the difference between the samples is attributed entirely to detector effects. Normalization is absorbed by using the observed event counts.
