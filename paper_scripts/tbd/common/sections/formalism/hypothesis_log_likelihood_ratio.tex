\paragraph{Hypothesis Log Likelihood Ratio}

The estimated probability densities determine the likelihood of each dataset through Eq.~\ref{eq:log_likelihood_of_a_learnable_distribution}. The likelihood of a hypothesis is the product of the likelihoods for both samples in both regions:

\begin{wideequations}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\likelihood(\hypothesis{i} | \dataset{A}, \dataset{B})
    &= \likelihood(\hypothesis{i}|\srdataset{A}, \srdataset{B})
       \cdot \likelihood(\hypothesis{i}|\crdataset{A}, \crdataset{B}) \\
    &= \likelihood(\srdist{\dataset{A}}^i|\srdataset{A})
       \cdot \likelihood(\srdist{\dataset{B}}^i|\srdataset{B}) \\
    &\quad \cdot \likelihood(\crdist{\dataset{A}}^i|\crdataset{A})
       \cdot \likelihood(\crdist{\dataset{B}}^i|\crdataset{B})
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\noindent for $i=0,1$. Comparing the hypotheses using the inverse log-likelihood ratio of Eq.~\ref{eq:inverse_log_likelihood_ratio} gives

\begin{equation*}
\invllratio = 2 \log \left(
    \frac{
        \max\limits_{f, \nparam} (\likelihood(\hypothesis{1}|\dataset{A}, \dataset{B})
    }{
        \max\limits_{\nparam} (\likelihood(\nullhypothesis|\dataset{A}, \dataset{B})
}\right).
\end{equation*}

\noindent Since $\log$ is monotonically increasing, this can be written as

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:high_level_overall_test_statistic}
    \invllratio = 2 \left( {\max\limits_{f, \nparam}(\log \likelihood(\hypothesis{1}|\dataset{A}, \dataset{B})} - {\max\limits_{\nparam}(\log \likelihood(\nullhypothesis|\dataset{A}, \dataset{B})} \right),
\end{equation}
\end{wideequations}

\noindent The full derivation is given in Appendix~\ref{Appendix_Tvalue}.
