\paragraph{Measuring Systematic Uncertainties}

To distinguish detector effects, described by $\nparam$, from a signal, we constrain them using signal-free data. We introduce an additional pair of samples, referred to as the \gls{cr}. The original pair defines the \gls{sr}, to which the new physics contribution is assumed to be confined. The sample notation is summarized in Table~\ref{table:division_of_data}.

\iftoggle{ispaper}{\begin{table}[htbp]}{\begin{table*}[t]}
\centering
\begin{ruledtabular}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
 & $\dataset{A}$ & $\dataset{B}$ \\
\hline
SR & $\dataset{A_{SR}}$ & $\dataset{B_{SR}}$ \\
CR & $\dataset{A_{CR}}$ & $\dataset{B_{CR}}$ \\
\end{tabular}
\end{ruledtabular}
\caption{Division of available data from different cuts into subsets for signal and control regions.}
\label{table:division_of_data}
\iftoggle{ispaper}{\end{table}}{\end{table*}}

The \gls{cr} must satisfy the following requirements:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The CR contains no new physics effects.
    \item The detector acts on \gls{sr} and \gls{cr} events in the same way as a function of $\anyevent$. In particular, the observable ranges are identical.
    \item All samples are disjoint.
\end{enumerate}

The \gls{cr} samples constrain the nuisance parameters that also enter the \gls{sr} hypotheses.
