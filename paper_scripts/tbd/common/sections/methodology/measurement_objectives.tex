To evaluate the sensitivity of the method, we repeatedly compare sample pairs with and without injected signals of different shapes and strengths. For each run, we load or generate disjoint signal-region and control-region sample pairs, inject a signal into the first signal-region sample when required, and construct a reference sample by combining the two samples in each region. We fit the alternative and null hypotheses by minimizing their respective loss functions, with the signal function fixed to zero under the null hypothesis and nuisance parameters fitted when enabled. The minimized losses determine the test statistic through Eq.~\ref{eq:t_value_from_two_losses}.

Repeating the procedure yields a sample of $t$ values whose distribution should approach $\chi^2_r$ in the limit of many repetitions. We compare the empirical and theoretical distributions in the plots presented below.

For generated data, the background-only distribution of $t$ can be estimated directly. For loaded data, we estimate it by combining $\dataset{A}$ and $\dataset{B}$ within each region, including any signal, and repeatedly repartitioning the events into samples of the original sizes. Each sample then follows, on average, the statistics of the combined reference distribution. The signal contribution therefore no longer produces a statistical difference between the samples.

We repeat the procedure for signals of different shapes and strengths. The significances corresponding to the median and the $\pm \sigma$ values of the resulting distribution are evaluated relative to both the theoretical and empirical background-only distributions.
