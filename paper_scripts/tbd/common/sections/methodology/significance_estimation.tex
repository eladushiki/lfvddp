\paragraph{Effect of Existing Signal}

During optimization, the flexible signal parametrization fits the most likely signal-like deviation arising from Poisson fluctuations, even in the absence of a true signal. The distribution of these fluctuations is described by Wilks and Wald and is closely related to the look-elsewhere effect.

A signal in either sample that can be represented by the signal parametrization should favor the signal hypothesis in the likelihood-ratio test (Eq.~\ref{eq:inverse_log_likelihood_ratio}), yielding larger $t$ values than in the background-only case. We quantify rejection of the background-only hypothesis by the significance

\begin{equation}
    Z = \Phi^{-1}(1-p)
\end{equation}

\noindent $\Phi$ being the inverse of the cumulative distribution of the Gaussian, $p$ is the p-value to get an equally or greater incompatibility with the background only hypothesis from a true background only distribution.

\paragraph{Significance Estimation}

We estimate the significance of a $\invllratio$ value in several ways.


    \paragraph{Theoretic significance from PDFs.}

    When analytic expressions for the PDFs are known, the significance is

    \begin{wideequations}
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:analytic_significance_from_pdfs}
        Z_\text{analytic} = \sqrt{\invllratio} = \sqrt  {
            2 \left( -N_s + \int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{k}} d\anyevent (N_b(\anyevent) + N_s(\anyevent)) \log \dfrac{N_s(\anyevent) + N_b(\anyevent)}{N_b(\anyevent)} \right)
        }
    \end{equation}
\end{wideequations}

    with $N_s$ the \gls{ndf} of signal events at a point $\event$ and $N_b$ the number of background events.

    \paragraph{Binned evident significance from run parameters.}

    When the \glspl{ndf} are not necessarily known, but the mean event counts are specified in generating the datasets, the expression can be written as

    \begin{wideequations}
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:binwise_significacne}
        Z_\text{evident} = \sqrt{\invllratio} = \sqrt{
            2 \left( -N_s + \sum\limits_{\event} (N_b(\event) + N_s(\event)) \log \dfrac{N_s(\event) + N_b(\event)}{N_b(\event)} \right)
        }
    \end{equation}
\end{wideequations}

\paragraph{Black box relative to $\chi^2$ or shuffle distribution.}

The distribution of $\invllratio$ can be estimated from its theoretical form or empirically by repeating the experiment with shuffled sample pairs. Comparing a single $\invllratio$ value with this distribution gives its discovery significance. In practice, we repeat the procedure and evaluate the significance of the mean.
