\subsubsection{Hypothesis Log Likelihood Ratio}

Given our estimation for the appearing probability densities of the datasets we can get a likelihood value for each of them from Equation~\ref{eq:log_likelihood_of_a_learnable_distribution}. To estimate the likelihood of a hypothesis, we should multiply all its distributions' likelihoods in both regions:

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\likelihood(\hypothesis{i} | \dataset{A}, \dataset{B})
    &= \likelihood(\hypothesis{i}|\srdataset{A}, \srdataset{B})
       \cdot \likelihood(\hypothesis{i}|\crdataset{A}, \crdataset{B}) \\
    &= \likelihood(\srdist{\dataset{A}}^i|\srdataset{A})
       \cdot \likelihood(\srdist{\dataset{B}}^i|\srdataset{B}) \\
    &\quad \cdot \likelihood(\crdist{\dataset{A}}^i|\crdataset{A})
       \cdot \likelihood(\crdist{\dataset{B}}^i|\crdataset{B})
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\noindent for $i=0,1$. Then, to compare between the likelihood of both hypotheses, we use the inverse log likelihood ratio, as in Eq.~\ref{eq:inverse_log_likelihood_ratio}, to get:

\begin{equation*}
\invllratio = 2 \log \left(
    \frac{
        \max\limits_{f, g, \nparam} (\likelihood(\hypothesis{1}|\dataset{A}, \dataset{B})
    }{
        \max\limits_{\nparam} (\likelihood(\nullhypothesis|\dataset{A}, \dataset{B})
}\right).
\end{equation*}

\noindent Since $\log$ is a monotonically increasing function, we have that

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:high_level_overall_test_statistic}
    \invllratio = 2 \left( {\max\limits_{f, g, \nparam}(\log \likelihood(\hypothesis{1}|\dataset{A}, \dataset{B})} - {\max\limits_{\nparam}(\log \likelihood(\nullhypothesis|\dataset{A}, \dataset{B})} \right),
\end{equation}

\noindent the calculation of which is fully carried out in Appendix~\ref{Appendix_Tvalue}).
